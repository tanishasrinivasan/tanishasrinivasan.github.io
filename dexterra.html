Dexterra Grip

A $200 assistive glove we built with, and for, one person.

Role
Hardware lead
Team
3 people, Project Invent
Timeline
Jan 2025 – July 2026
Tools
Arduino, SolidWorks, 3D printing

Who it's for

Our community partner has weak grip and limited dexterity after paralysis. Commercial assistive gloves cost about ten times more than what we ended up building.

How it works

Force-sensitive resistors read what he's trying to do, and an Arduino Nano drives a servo that pulls tendons along his fingers, adding up to 30 N of grip. We tuned the control for each finger in software, because an equal pull on every finger didn't match how his hand actually moves.

Five designs in six months

We tested with him every week for six months. Between versions we:

  • Rerouted the tendons
  • Moved the servo from his palm to his wrist
  • Switched from cheap parts to heavy-duty ones and changed the glove material
  • Made the SolidWorks-designed 3D-printed parts smaller and lighter each time

What changed for him

With the final glove he can hold a cup, open a jar, hold a water bottle, press the buttons on his hair dryer, turn on the sink and play guitar.

Beyond the build

We drop-tested it, checked battery life and wore it for full days to test comfort. I did the cost breakdown, we talked with therapists, and we pitched it to judges. Dexterra was a U.S. finalist at the InventFuture.Global Innovation Challenge, out of about 20 teams, and won the Project Invent Moonshot Award.
